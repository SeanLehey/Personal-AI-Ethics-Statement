# Personal AI Ethics Statement

#### Where I <ins>Don't</ins> Use AI Tools:
I don't use LLMs to do my writing for me. I understand that LLMs can be a useful tool for helping with writing and editing, but I prefer to communicate without their assistance. As a former editor, I've developed my own voice and therefore don't see the need to offload my writing to an LLM.

#### Where I <ins>Do</ins> Use AI Tools:
I use LLMs to help me learn how to code. It's my goal to understand what the LLM is teaching me, and I make sure to avoid blindly copying and pasting LLM-generated code output into my projects. An LLM should teach by example, but I still have an obligation to comprehend what it's doing and why. I plan and outline scripts, interpret what is produced, and adjust them accordingly. It seems that as LLMs become more robust at coding tasks, our role as humans is shifting more toward the QA side of things, though it still behooves us to understand what we're putting out there. 

#### Why I Wrote an Ethics Statement:
Tools like LLMs represent a huge step forward in education, business, and other fields. But I'm personally concerned about people becoming overly reliant on LLMs, especially once it gets to the point of supplanting one's own agency and ability to think critically. I think they make a great learning companion and multitool, but I'm hesitant to outsource all of my cognition to this technology. I think the best approach is to incorporate LLMs as another tool in our arsenal while staying informed about their strengths, limitations, and dangers as the technology evolves over time.
