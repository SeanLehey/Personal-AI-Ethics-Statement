# Personal AI Ethics Statement

#### Where I <ins>Don't</ins> Use AI Tools:
I don't use LLMs to do my writing for me. I wouldn't want to interact with a facsimile of someone's thoughts, and I'm sure many other people feel the same way. To pass off an LLM's writings as my own would be a disservice to myself and the person I'm communicating with.

#### Where I <ins>Do</ins> Use AI Tools:
I use LLMs to help me learn how to code. It's my goal to understand what the LLM is teaching me, and I make sure to avoid blindly copying and pasting LLM-generated code output into my projects. An LLM should teach by example, but I still have an obligation to comprehend what it's doing and why.

#### Why This Matters:
Tools like LLMs represent a huge step forward in education, business, and other fields. But I'm personally concerned about people becoming overly reliant on LLMs, especially once it gets to the point of supplanting one's own agency and ability to think critically. I think they make a great learning companion and multitool, but I'm hesitant to outsource all of my cognition to this technology. I think the best approach is to incorporate LLMs as another tool in our arsenal while staying informed about their strengths, limitations, and dangers as the technology evolves over time.
